The bench is short-staffed. The paperwork isn't.

New hires arrive with very different levels of preparation, preceptors are stretched thin, and competency documentation still has to be complete and on time for every person and every test system.

01

Training eats supervisor time

Every new tech needs QC, calibration, maintenance and troubleshooting taught again from scratch, usually by whoever has a spare minute between runs.

02

Competency lives in binders and spreadsheets

Six assessment elements per test system, semiannual in the first year and annual after that. It's easy for one signature or one expiry date to slip.

03

Troubleshooting depends on who's on shift

When QC shifts, one tech checks the reagent, another recalibrates, a third reruns controls until they pass. The investigation and the documentation vary with the person.

Built from 40 years of real bench work

I've spent nearly four decades as a medical laboratory technologist: running chemistry, working up QC failures, bringing new analysers online, doing lot-to-lot studies and correlations, and training the people who came after me.

A software company can build a dashboard, and an academic can explain the theory. What's hard to manufacture is knowing what to check first when Level 3 glucose is out and Level 1 is fine.

That's what LabReady Pro is made of.

Sample case · QC Troubleshooting “MultiQual Level 3 is +2.4 SD for glucose on Cobas Pure 1. Level 1 is within range. Patients are queued.”
  1. Hold patient results for the affected run
  2. Concentration-dependent? Check calibration and the upper curve
  3. Control material: vial, reconstitution, open date, storage
  4. Reagent: lot, on-board stability, recent changes
  5. Maintenance log, error flags, environment
  6. Rerun, recalibrate if indicated, verify with fresh QC
  7. Look back at patients since last acceptable QC
  8. Document corrective action and follow-up

Questions

Is LabReady Pro affiliated with CAP, CLIA or COLA?

No. LabReady Pro provides original training and documentation tools. It helps you organise evidence and workflows around the requirements that apply to your lab, and it doesn't reproduce any accreditor's licensed checklist content. Always work from your accrediting body's current official materials.

Does this replace our procedures or the manufacturer's instructions?

No. Your SOPs, package inserts and operator manuals remain the authority. LabReady Pro can be customised to reference your own procedures, and your laboratory director keeps responsibility for approving competency content.

Can we customise the competency packages?

Yes. Every package, quiz and observation form can be edited to match your test menu, instruments and procedures.

What about the AI assistant and patient care?

The AI assistant is for training, documentation and decision support. It won't make patient-care decisions, and users remain responsible for following their laboratory's procedures and applicable regulations.

We don't run Roche or Abbott. Is it still useful?

Yes. The Chemistry Competency System is instrument-neutral. Instrument packs are an add-on, and the pilot form tells me which analysers to prioritise next.
